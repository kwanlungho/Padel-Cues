Match-day reminders

Padel Cues

Simple cues, not technical overload. Pick one or two ideas before you step on court and let them guide the point.

Before you step on court

Small steps, get set
Firm wrist, compact volley
Side-on, balanced, reach high
Footwork

Small steps. Get set.

Adjust with your feet rather than reaching. Arrive balanced before you hit.

Ready position

Split as they hit.

Time the split step to your opponent's contact so you can react in either direction.

Balance

Soft knees. Light feet.

Stay athletic and ready to move instead of becoming upright and planted.

Preparation

Track the ball. Turn early.

Read the flight first, then prepare the body before the ball reaches you.

Grip

Continental grip. Relax your hand.

Keep the grip simple and avoid squeezing the racket harder than the shot needs.

Non-playing hand

Guide, then reset.

Use the free hand for spacing and balance, then return to a ready position.

Volley

Firm wrist. Compact swing.

Let the incoming pace do the work. Keep the racket stable and the action short.

Volley

Contact in front. Finish towards the target.

Meet the ball ahead of you and guide it through the intended line.

Volley footwork

Step into the volley when there's time.

Move through the ball under control rather than reaching from a static base.

Serve

Controlled slice. Place it, then move in.

Prioritise a reliable serve and use it to establish your position at the net.

Glass

Read the rebound. Create space.

Don't crowd the glass. Give the ball room to come out before making contact.

Smash preparation

Turn side-on. Get behind the ball.

Move early so the ball stays in front of your hitting shoulder rather than drifting behind you.

Smash power

Load the legs. Drive upwards.

Create power from the ground and body instead of trying to manufacture it only with the arm.

Smash contact

Reach high without losing balance.

Attack the highest comfortable contact point while staying controlled enough to recover.

Smash finish

Let the body follow through. Recover.

Finish naturally, then get ready for the next ball instead of admiring the shot.
